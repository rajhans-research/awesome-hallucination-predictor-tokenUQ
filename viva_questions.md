## Pillar 1: Theoretical Foundations & Problem Formulation

### Q1: What is the core difference between token-level uncertainty and semantic uncertainty?
**Answer:** Token-level uncertainty measures the dispersion or likelihood of the model's conditional probability distribution at each individual autoregressive decoding step ($P(y_t \mid \mathbf{x}, y_{<t})$) in lexical space. In contrast, semantic uncertainty (Kuhn et al., ICLR 2023) measures the dispersion across distinct meaning spaces by grouping multiple stochastically sampled generation sequences into bidirectional entailment equivalence classes. Token uncertainty operates in $O(1)$ generation passes on a single generated text, whereas semantic uncertainty requires $N$ stochastic generation passes plus $O(N^2)$ cross-encoder natural language inference (NLI) checks.

### Q2: What is "syntactic dilution", and how does it degrade standard token log-probability averages?
**Answer:** Autoregressive language models generate both content-bearing lexical items (named entities, dates, domain verbs) and grammatical function words (articles like *"the"*, auxiliary verbs like *"was"*, prepositions like *"in"*). Function words are heavily constrained by local syntax and language priors, causing the model to emit them with near-zero negative log-probabilities (often $< 0.05$). When computing a naive sentence mean ($\frac{1}{M}\sum -\ln P(y_t)$), these highly confident syntactic filler tokens deflate the average score of a sentence containing fabricated factual entities, causing false negatives in hallucination detection.

### Q3: Why is minimum token probability (maximum negative logprob) often a stronger predictor of factual errors than mean token probability?
**Answer:** Factual hallucinations frequently hinge on a single fabricated atomic detail—for example, generating *"1972"* instead of *"1968"*, or an invented university name—within an otherwise grammatically fluent and factually correct sentence. While token averaging dilutes this isolated error across 25 sentence tokens, the minimum token probability ($\min_t P(y_t) \iff \max_t [-\ln P(y_t)]$) directly isolates the single point of maximum model hesitation or surprise, capturing span-level factual disruptions.

### Q4: How do you mathematically define the top-$K$ predictive entropy at decoding step $t$?
**Answer:** Let $\mathbf{z}_t$ be the logits at step $t$, and let $\mathcal{V}_K = \{w_1, \dots, w_K\}$ represent the top-$K$ candidate tokens ($K=5$). We first re-normalize the truncated probabilities over this support:
$$\tilde{P}(w_i) = \frac{\exp(z_{t, i})}{\sum_{j=1}^K \exp(z_{t, j})}$$
The top-$K$ predictive entropy is then computed as:
$$\mathcal{H}_K(y_t) = -\sum_{i=1}^K \tilde{P}(w_i) \ln \tilde{P}(w_i)$$
Sentence-level entropy is obtained by averaging $\mathcal{H}_K(y_t)$ across all sentence tokens.

### Q5: What is a "High-Certainty Hallucination" as formalized by Simhi et al. (2024)?
**Answer:** A high-certainty hallucination occurs when an LLM outputs factually incorrect or fabricated claims with high output confidence (low token negative log-probabilities or high softmax probabilities). This typically stems from parametric memorization errors, spurious training correlations, or over-tuning on unverified web corpora. Because the internal likelihood remains high, standard uncertainty quantification metrics completely fail to flag these sentences.

### Q6: What was the primary research question (RQ1) investigated in your study?
**Answer:** RQ1 investigated the Cost-Quality Pareto Frontier: *Can lightweight, single-pass token uncertainty signals match or exceed the factual hallucination detection quality of multi-pass black-box sampling consistency while requiring zero additional LLM generation passes?*

## Pillar 2: Dataset, Alignment, and Security Engineering

### Q7: Why did you select the `potsawee/wiki_bio_gpt3_hallucination` dataset instead of TruthfulQA or HaluEval?
**Answer:** WikiBio GPT-3 contains 238 full biographies generated by `text-davinci-003` with 1,908 sentence-level annotations across three fine-grained human labels (`accurate`, `minor_inaccurate`, `major_inaccurate`), paired with 20 stochastic completions per passage ($N=20$) and exact token log-probabilities. TruthfulQA is short-form multiple-choice/QA, which does not evaluate long-form narrative hallucinations. HaluEval largely relies on synthetic perturbations (ChatGPT-injected errors) rather than natural, unprompted LLM hallucinations annotated by human domain experts.

### Q8: Describe the multi-byte UTF-8 token alignment failure you discovered in existing open-source code and how you resolved it.
**Answer:** Standard open-source notebooks (including the baseline notebook from SelfCheckGPT) attempt to align human-annotated sentences to GPT-3 BPE tokens by joining tokens into strings (`"".join(tokens)`) and searching for substring matches. When passages contain special characters, diacritics, or non-ASCII letters (e.g., Faroese letters in Passage 30773: *"Týr"*, *"Sirið Stenberg"*), byte-pair decoders emit replacement characters or multi-byte shifts, causing string matching to fail and dropping sentences. We eliminated this by writing an exact character-coordinate span aligner in `src/data_loader.py` that utilizes OpenAI API `text_offset` integer pointers relative to `choices[0]['text']`, achieving 100.0% sentence-to-token alignment (1,908/1,908 sentences).

### Q9: What security vulnerability did you discover in the raw benchmark `.bin` files, and what remediation was applied?
**Answer:** The binary `.bin` files from the author's public repository were pickled using legacy OpenAI SDK v0.27 objects. Inspecting the raw object state revealed that `_init_args` stored the original API keys (`sk-...`) used during generation. To enforce absolute security hygiene, we implemented `_SafeOpenAIObject` in `src/data_loader.py` to intercept deserialization and permanently discard `_init_args`, sanitized the data into a pure JSON schema (`wikibio_processed.json`), deleted the raw `.bin` directories from disk, and enforced git exclusion via `.gitignore`.

### Q10: How do the three factual error evaluation protocols (`nonfact`, `nonfact_star`, `major_only`) differ?
**Answer:**
1. `NonFact` ($N=1,908$, PosRate $= 72.96\%$): Evaluates general factuality by treating any error (`minor_inaccurate` $\cup$ `major_inaccurate`) as positive (1) vs. `accurate` as negative (0).
2. `Major_Only` ($N=1,277$, PosRate $= 59.59\%$): Evaluates severe fabrications by taking `major_inaccurate` as positive (1) and `accurate` as negative (0), excluding ambiguous minor slips.
3. `NonFact*` ($N=1,632$, PosRate $= 29.72\%$): Evaluates low-base-rate detection by treating `minor_inaccurate` as factual background noise (`major_inaccurate` as 1 vs. `accurate` $\cup$ `minor_inaccurate` as 0).

### Q11: How did you prevent data leakage in your experimental design?
**Answer:** Our evaluation is entirely unsupervised and zero-resource. Neither the baseline methods nor our proposed keyword-filtered and hybrid detectors fit learned parameters via gradient descent on the benchmark text. Our keyword stopword list ($\mathcal{W}_{\text{stop}}$) is a standard closed syntactic vocabulary from computational linguistics, and the linear fusion parameter $\alpha=0.70$ is frozen across all passages.

### Q12: Why is AUC-PR necessary alongside AUROC on this benchmark?
**Answer:** AUROC evaluates the True Positive Rate against the False Positive Rate across all thresholds and is invariant to class distribution. However, on skewed datasets—such as the `NonFact*` protocol where the positive rate drops to $29.72\%$—AUROC can present an overly optimistic assessment because a high number of true negatives suppresses FPR. AUC-PR directly plots Precision against Recall, penalizing false positive spikes in low-prevalence regimes.

## Pillar 3: Proposed Methods & Algorithmic Design

### Q13: Explain the exact formulation and intuition behind `token_keyword`.
**Answer:** `token_keyword` isolates content-bearing lexical items from grammatical filler. For each sentence $s_k$, we filter the token index set $\mathcal{I}_{\text{content}}(s_k) = \{ t \in [t_{\text{start}}, t_{\text{end}}] \mid \text{lemma}(y_t) \notin \mathcal{W}_{\text{stop}} \land \text{is\_alpha}(y_t) \}$. The score is the mean negative log-probability computed exclusively over content tokens:
$$\phi_{\text{keyword}}(s_k) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{\text{content}}(s_k)|} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{I}_{\text{content}}(s_k)} \left( -\ln P(y_t \mid \mathbf{x}, y_{<t}) \right)$$
This strips out predictable function words, amplifying the uncertainty signal localized in proper nouns, dates, and domain verbs.

### Q14: What is `token_hybrid`, and why was $\alpha=0.70$ selected?
**Answer:** `token_hybrid` is a convex linear fusion of sequence-level content uncertainty and extreme-value local hesitation:
$$\phi_{\text{hybrid}}(s_k; \alpha) = \alpha \cdot \phi_{\text{keyword}}(s_k) + (1 - \alpha) \cdot \phi_{\text{min}}(s_k)$$
Evaluating $\alpha \in \{0.50, 0.70\}$ revealed that $\alpha=0.70$ achieves the highest AUROC ($74.93\%$) and lowest FPR@95 ($65.50\%$). Weighting content-word mean uncertainty at $70\%$ establishes a stable baseline across the sentence, while allocating $30\%$ to maximum negative logprob ensures that isolated factual slips trigger an alert.

### Q15: How does your implementation of `SelfCheck-1gram` construct its language model?
**Answer:** For a given passage, `SelfCheck-1gram` takes the $N$ stochastically sampled completions $\mathbf{p}^{(1)}, \dots, \mathbf{p}^{(N)}$, tokenizes them into lowercase unigram counts using a blank English tokenizer, and fits an empirical maximum likelihood probability distribution with laplacian or empirical frequency smoothing. For each test sentence $s_k$, it evaluates the unigram probability of each token and returns the maximum negative log-probability ($-\ln P_{\text{samples}}(w)$) across the sentence.

### Q16: How did you resolve the infinite penalty crash on `SelfCheck-2gram` when $N=2$?
**Answer:** When evaluating bigrams across only 2 sampled passages, unseen test bigrams receive zero frequency ($P(g) = 0.0$). Taking $\ln(0.0)$ produces $-\infty$, which inverts to $+\infty$ for negative log-probability. In scikit-learn, functions like `precision_recall_curve` execute `assert_all_finite()`, which raises a `ValueError`. We resolved this by applying numerical clamping via `np.nan_to_num(y_score, posinf=50.0, neginf=-50.0)` in `src/evaluation.py`. This assigns the maximum finite anomaly score to absent n-grams, preserving their top-ranked status without corrupting numerical stability.

### Q17: What is the computational complexity of `token_hybrid` versus `SelfCheck-1gram` with $N=20$?
**Answer:**
- `token_hybrid`: Requires 1 LLM generation pass ($T$ tokens). Post-processing scans the token array in $O(M)$ time using a hash-set stopword lookup ($O(1)$ per token). On our host, post-processing executes in **$0.63\text{ ms}$** per passage.
- `SelfCheck-1gram (N=20)`: Requires 21 LLM generation passes ($1$ prompt pass + $20$ stochastic samples $\approx 20 \times 150 = 3,000$ generated tokens). Post-processing requires parsing 20 passages, fitting unigram frequency tables, and scoring the test sentence, taking **$49.23\text{ ms}$** on CPU ($78\times$ slower post-processing, plus seconds of autoregressive LLM inference).

### Q18: Did you evaluate top-5 entropy production rate, and how did it perform?
**Answer:** Yes, `token_entropy5` was evaluated across all protocols. On `NonFact`, it achieved an AUROC of $67.67\%$ [95% CI: $64.91, 70.57$], which is lower than `token_mean` ($71.66\%$) and `token_min` ($73.79\%$). In commercial APIs, output logits are truncated to top-5 tokens. Normalizing entropy over only 5 tokens underestimates predictive dispersion when the true distribution has a long tail, explaining why direct negative log-probability of the chosen token is a superior hallucination predictor.

## Pillar 4: Baseline Validation & Computational Optimizations

### Q19: How did you achieve a 50x speedup in your Gate G2 baseline validation?
**Answer:** Profiling the official `selfcheckgpt` package revealed that `UnigramModel.__init__` repeatedly invoked `spacy.load("en_core_web_sm")` inside the passage loop (238 disk reloads), executing full neural dependency parsing and NER models simply to tokenize words. This stalled execution for $>8$ minutes. We optimized this in `src/baseline_methods.py` by caching a lightweight `spacy.blank("en")` tokenizer with a sentencizer. We verified exact numerical parity against the uncached neural parser on Passage 0 (`[4.6485, 4.3993, 4.6913, 4.5689]`), slashing runtime from 500+ seconds to **8.87 seconds**.

### Q20: What were your Gate G2 validation results, and how did they compare to published figures?
**Answer:** In Gate G2, our reproduced baseline achieved:
- `selfcheck_1gram_max_N20`: AUROC = **$85.63\%$** (Exact 0.00% match with Manakul et al. 2023, Table 1).
- `selfcheck_1gram_avg_N20`: AUROC = **$81.52\%$** (Exact 0.00% match with Manakul et al. 2023, Table 1).
- `token_mean`: AUC-PR = **$84.01\%$** (versus $83.21\%$ in Manakul's open-source notebook; our $+0.80\%$ gain resulted from eliminating the BPE alignment drops).

### Q21: How were the 95% confidence intervals computed?
**Answer:** We implemented non-parametric empirical percentile bootstrapping with $B=1,000$ resamples in `src/evaluation.py`. At each iteration, $N$ test sentence pairs $(y_{\text{true}}, y_{\text{score}})$ are sampled with replacement using fixed random seed `42`. AUROC and AUC-PR are computed on each replicate, and the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the empirical bootstrap distribution define the lower and upper bounds.

### Q22: Why did you freeze the random seed to 42 throughout the pipeline?
**Answer:** Freezing random seed `42` across dataset partitioning, bootstrap resamples, and multi-pass subsampling sweeps guarantees 100% deterministic reproducibility. Any researcher executing `python scripts/run_experiment.py` will generate metric CSVs and figures identical down to the last decimal place.

## Pillar 5: Experimental Findings, Pareto Analysis & High-Certainty Autopsy

### Q23: What are the main findings from your Cost-Quality Pareto Frontier analysis (Fig. 3)?
**Answer:**
1. The Pareto-optimal frontier is exclusively defined by single-pass methods: `token_mean` ($0.13\text{ ms}$, $71.66\%$), `token_min` ($0.18\text{ ms}$, $73.79\%$), and `token_hybrid_a70` ($0.63\text{ ms}$, $74.93\%$).
2. Low-budget multi-pass sampling ($N=2, 3, 5$) is strictly dominated by single-pass methods: for instance, `SelfCheck-1gram` with $N=5$ requires 5 extra generation passes and $16.26\text{ ms}$ latency but only achieves $70.66\%$ AUROC, which is $4.27\%$ lower than single-pass `token_hybrid_a70`.
3. To match or slightly exceed single-pass hybrid UQ, multi-pass consistency requires at least 20 stochastic samples ($N=20$), incurring a $78\times$ post-processing latency penalty and a 20-fold increase in generation expenditure.

### Q24: Why does `SelfCheck-TFIDF` outperform token methods on `Major_Only` ($81.07\%$ vs $75.73\%$), but underperform on `NonFact` ($71.71\%$ vs $74.93\%$)?
**Answer:** On the `NonFact` benchmark, many errors are subtle (`minor_inaccurate`), such as an incorrect date, altered middle name, or distorted geographic location. These minor factual errors manifest as localized token hesitation (caught by `token_min` and `token_hybrid`), but do not significantly shift passage-wide TF-IDF word vectors. Conversely, under `Major_Only`, errors are severe fabrications (e.g., inventing an entirely fictional career), causing large lexical divergences across sampled passages that TF-IDF cosine distance detects with high precision.

### Q25: Describe the setup and results of your Deeper Analysis 2 on High-Certainty Hallucinations.
**Answer:** We isolated $N=191$ sentences labeled `major_inaccurate` where `token_mean` was in the bottom quartile of uncertainty for fabricated text ($\le 0.3919$). We set operating thresholds for each detector corresponding to $80\%$ overall recall across the entire dataset, and evaluated what fraction of these 191 confident falsehoods each method detected:
- `token_mean` detected 71/191 (**$37.2\%$ recall**).
- `token_keyword` detected 69/191 (**$36.1\%$ recall**).
- `token_min` detected 81/191 (**$42.4\%$ recall**).
- `SelfCheck-1gram` ($N=20$) recovered 141/191 (**$73.8\%$ recall**).
- `SelfCheck-TFIDF` ($N=20$) recovered 156/191 (**$81.7\%$ recall**).

### Q26: What is the fundamental scientific takeaway from the High-Certainty Autopsy?
**Answer:** Internal token uncertainty and external sampling consistency possess **orthogonal failure boundaries**. When an LLM suffers from strong parametric bias, it generates falsehoods with high token probabilities, completely blinding internal UQ detectors. However, stochastic sampling at $T > 0$ forces the generator onto alternative paths, exposing the instability of the memorized fact. This proves that sampling consistency is not a replacement for token UQ, but an orthogonal verification layer.

### Q27: How does your qualitative case study on Passage 0 (John Peter Russell) illustrate this phenomenon?
**Answer:** In Passage 0, GPT-3 generated: *"He was educated at the Sydney Grammar School and then studied art at the Slade School of Fine Art in London"* (`major_inaccurate`). Because the Slade School is strongly associated with Victorian painters in web text, the model emitted the tokens with high probability ($\text{token\_mean} = 0.3918$), evading single-pass detection. However, across 20 stochastic samples, alternative generations cited *"Royal Academy"*, *"Julian Academy"*, and *"East London School"*. The lexical divergence produced a high TF-IDF inconsistency score ($0.960$), successfully catching the hallucination.

### Q28: What is your proposed "Tiered Deployment Architecture" for production LLMs?
**Answer:** Rather than choosing exclusively between single-pass UQ and multi-pass consistency, we propose a two-tier cascading pipeline:
- **Tier 1 (Streaming Gate):** Every generated sentence is evaluated in real time via single-pass `token_hybrid_a70` ($0.63\text{ ms}$, zero extra passes). Obvious factual errors are flagged immediately.
- **Tier 2 (Audit Gate):** Outputs where Tier 1 scores fall in an ambiguous boundary or outputs destined for high-stakes decisions are conditionally routed to multi-pass consistency checking (`SelfCheck-TFIDF`). This achieves maximal Pareto efficiency: $90\%$ of traffic is checked at zero marginal cost, while expensive stochastic sampling is reserved for high-risk cases.

### Q29: What are the main limitations of your study?
**Answer:** As acknowledged in Section IX of the paper:
1. Evaluation was conducted on biography generation (WikiBio). While canonical for factual hallucination benchmarking, multi-hop mathematical reasoning or code generation may exhibit different entropy patterns.
2. The approach requires grey-box access to top-5 output log-probabilities, which is available in commercial developer APIs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Bedrock) but unavailable in consumer chat interfaces where probabilities are stripped.
3. The fusion weight $\alpha=0.70$ was statically frozen rather than dynamically adapted per sentence.
